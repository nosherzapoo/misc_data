# Liberty Media (FWONK): The commercial flywheel is compounding into the 2Q print

Liberty reports 2Q26 in early August against a quarter we already expect to be the calendar trough (5 races vs. 9 in 2Q25 on the Middle East cancellations, with our Adj. OIBDA down ~18% Y/Y). The investment debate, however, sits in the commercial lines, and the dataflow since our May note has been uniformly constructive. We update the sponsorship thesis with the 2Q deal tape, size the licensing opportunity the market is not modeling, and work through the three European media renewals now in motion: Spain (live), Germany (loaded), and Austria (resolved).

## Sponsorship: the lag keeps compressing, and the whitespace keeps closing

In May we argued the historical 2-year lag between a media rights step-up and sponsorship momentum was compressing at F1, and the subsequent tape has strengthened that case. The 2026 cohort now includes Standard Chartered (Official Wealth Management and CIB Partner, January), Marsh (Official Risk and Insurance Brokering Partner, April), the Salesforce extension to 2030, the Allwyn renewal, and FanDuel and Betway, with all six league deals signed before Apple's first US race aired. The quarter added activation proof points at the top of the inventory stack: Louis Vuitton took title of the Monaco GP in June under year two of LVMH's 10-year, ~$150M-per-year partnership, MSC Cruises held title of the Barcelona race, and F1 signed live-entertainment platform Fever as an Official Supplier in July (Exhibit A). We continue to see sponsorship approaching ~$1.1B by FY2027 and ~$1.2B by FY2028, holding roughly 30% of primary revenue, against a ~$268M base when Liberty took control in 2017 (Exhibit B).

The whitespace map is converting on schedule. Of the nine underpenetrated categories we sized at $120-220M in May, two have now closed: insurance, where Marsh becomes the sport's first league-level partner in a category we flagged as having zero presence against a $160M NFL healthcare comp, and betting, via FanDuel and Betway. Global telecom is partially addressed (T-Mobile remains US-regional), leaving ~$105-195M of identified whitespace across seven categories, with automotive still the most striking absence in the year Audi arrives as a works team and Cadillac joins the grid (Exhibit A). The forward catalysts are unchanged: ~$145M of renewals in 2027 (Rolex, Pirelli, MSC, Lenovo, Santander) as the first major post-Apple batch, and the ~$255M wall in 2030, where bull-case renewal pricing of 15-20% on US-active sponsors translates to ~$60-80M of incremental annual revenue. On the call we watch the pace of new logo announcements and any commentary on 2027 renewal negotiations beginning early, consistent with Domenicali's "renewing much more in advance" framing.

## Licensing: the unmodeled line

Licensing is the one commercial engine at F1 with no line in anyone's model, ours included, because the company reports it inside "other F1 revenue" alongside Paddock Club, F1 TV, freight and TV production. The anecdotal evidence says the base is inflecting. LEGO's CEO cited F1 sets among the innovations driving record first-half revenue and profit, LEGO's Speed Champions consumer sales grew 16% in 2025 against a 7% toy industry, and the brick-built kart parade at Silverstone this month became one of the most shared moments of the race weekend. Mattel is running staggered Hot Wheels F1 drops across 150+ countries, Disney's Mickey & Friends "Fuel the Magic" program runs through 2026-27 with distribution via DisneyStore.com, Topps and Pacsun both renewed, and F1 The Movie became Apple's highest-grossing theatrical release (~$630M worldwide) with a sequel in development. More than 4 million children aged 8-12 now actively follow F1 across the EU and US, an audience that did not exist five years ago and one that monetizes through toys and games long before it buys a race ticket.

Our bottom-up build, anchored to named programs across eight categories (games, construction toys, die-cast, apparel, trading cards, location-based entertainment, film, and family collabs), suggests a royalty base of roughly $80-150M today with a credible path toward $175-310M by 2030 (Exhibit C). At the midpoint that is ~$0.14 of licensing royalty per fan across F1's 827M-strong base, a fraction of what mature US leagues extract, which is precisely the point: this is the same under-monetization argument we have made on sponsorship, one derivative earlier. The strategic logic also feeds back into media: the movie halo was, in our view, a direct contributor to Apple paying $150M per year for US rights, roughly 1.7x the ESPN deal. We would not be surprised to see management give licensing its first dedicated airtime on this call or at an investor day, and any disclosure that isolates the line is an easy positive catalyst.

## European media renewals: Spain is live, Germany is loaded, Austria is done

Spain expires first and is the live negotiation. DAZN holds exclusive rights for 2024-2026 at a reported ~EUR 55M per year, and local press reports the renewal process is underway. What makes this cycle interesting is that the last one went backwards: DAZN's EUR 55M was a step down from the EUR 65M Telefonica paid from 2020-23, struck in a softer moment for the property. The 2027 cycle prices against a very different asset: two Spanish races from this season (the Madrid "Madring" street race debuts in September alongside Barcelona), Alonso and Sainz still on the grid, and a bidder set that includes DAZN (incumbent, with a deepened Movistar Plus+ distribution alliance), Telefonica itself, and Mediaset on the FTA side. We see EUR 65-80M per year as the credible renewal zone, a 20-45% uplift and simply a recovery to, then past, the prior cycle's high (Exhibit D).

Germany is the loaded spring. Sky Deutschland's deal runs to end-2027 at a reported EUR 50-55M per year, and it was conspicuously the one major Sky market excluded when F1 renewed Sky Italia early to 2032 and Sky UK early to 2034 in May. We read the exclusion as deliberate on both sides: RTL's takeover of Sky Deutschland has now cleared the EU Commission, creating a merged buyer that uniquely combines pay distribution, the FTA reach that has been missing since RTL exited in 2020, and Ad Alliance monetization. F1 has no reason to price Germany before that entity is ready to pay for the full stack, particularly with Audi's works debut and Cadillac raising German-market relevance, and the German trade press is already tracking the gap week to week. Austria, meanwhile, resolved cleanly in the quarter: ServusTV extended free-to-air rights through 2029 on June 26, locking continuity around the home race, with the ORF sub-license for 2027-29 the only open detail. Taken together, the three markets represent ~EUR 120M of current AAV renewing into a market where the demonstrated pattern is early renewals at step-ups (US +1.7x via Apple; UK extended five years early), and our scenario math implies EUR 138-171M on renewal (Exhibit D). None of this is in 2026 numbers, and all of it supports the media rights growth assumptions embedded in our $115/sh target.

## What we watch on the call

Four things. First, the sponsorship growth rate and any new logo or early-renewal announcements, which validate the compressed-lag thesis for a second consecutive quarter. Second, any first disclosure or dedicated commentary on licensing, where the market carries no number at all. Third, Middle East mechanics tracking our base case: team payments recognized pro rata, the ~200bp team payment leverage guidance intact, and any single replacement race skewing outcomes toward our bull case. Fourth, media rights commentary on Spain and Germany, where even a "constructive discussions" signal would confirm the renewal cycle is running ahead of expiry, consistent with the UK and Italy playbook.
